1. Introduction
Food waste is one of the most overlooked sustainability problems of modern households. Globally, roughly one third of all food produced is lost or wasted, while many people cannot afford adequate nutrition. In recent years, researchers have explored digital applications as a tool to reduce this waste; however, studies show that users often abandon these apps because manually logging every food item becomes too burdensome over time (Mathisen & Johansen, 2022). This project, FreshTrack, proposes an AI-powered pantry and meal assistant that combines low-effort expiry tracking with photo-based ingredient recognition and automatic recipe suggestions, aiming to make waste reduction practical for everyday users.

2. Problem Statement
Households and small stores regularly discard edible food because expiry dates are forgotten and pantry contents are poorly tracked. As a result, people frequently buy ingredients they already own, struggle to decide what to cook from what they have, and ultimately throw food away. Existing food-tracking apps fail to keep users engaged due to their reliance on tedious manual entry. The problem this project addresses is the lack of an easy-to-use system that tracks what food a user has, warns them before items expire, and suggests meals based on those ingredients using AI to minimize the effort required from the user.
3. Motivation
This problem is worth solving for economic, environmental, and ethical reasons. Food waste costs families money and places unnecessary pressure on the environment through wasted resources and landfill emissions. On a personal level, I grew up being taught that throwing away edible food is wrong when so many people cannot afford to eat a value that has shaped this project's direction. Technically, the problem is also an excellent fit for my skills: ingredient recognition from photos is a well-studied task where transfer learning has achieved strong results with limited datasets (Morol et al., 2022), making a one-semester implementation realistic. Finally, research confirms that combining waste tracking with meal inspiration is exactly what users of existing apps said was missing (Mathisen & Johansen, 2022), which suggests genuine demand for this solution.
4. Project Objectives
The main objective is to design and implement a web application that reduces household food waste through intelligent pantry management. The specific objectives are:
Develop a pantry inventory system where users can add and remove items with expiry dates.
Implement reminder notifications that warn users before their food expires.
Build an ingredient detection feature that recognizes common ingredients (10–20 classes) from photos using transfer learning with MobileNetV2, targeting at least 85% classification accuracy.
Integrate a recipe suggestion feature that recommends meals based on the user's available ingredients, prioritizing items nearing expiry.
Log user actions to measure the system's impact on waste reduction.
5. Preliminaries
The first version of FreshTrack will include manual item entry, expiry reminders, photo-based ingredient recognition for a limited set of common ingredients, and recipe suggestions via an external recipe API. It will not include barcode scanning, grocery store integrations, nutrition or calorie tracking, or support for multiple users per household. The recipe database will not be built from scratch; instead, the Spoonacular API will be used for recipe search and ranking by ingredient match (Spoonacular, 2026). Ingredient recognition will be implemented using transfer learning rather than training a model from scratch, following the approach recommended in the TensorFlow documentation (TensorFlow, 2026).
